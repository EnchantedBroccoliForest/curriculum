# Week 9: Prince ch. 12: transformers

## Why this week

Attention and the transformer block are the one architecture the plan asks you to both build and derive closed-book. This week fixes the equations and their cost; [week 10](/week/10) and [week 11](/week/11) code them; [week 17](/week/17) tests backprop through the block from memory. The FLOP and memory arithmetic in sequence length is reused directly in the KV-cache and FlashAttention weeks of Phase 2.

## Objectives

- Write scaled dot-product attention, multi-head attention, and one pre-norm transformer block with every tensor's shape, from memory.
- Count the FLOPs and the activation memory of one attention layer as functions of sequence length $T$, model width $d$, and head count $H$, and say which term dominates when.
- Construct the causal mask for $T = 4$ by hand and state its effect on the softmax.

## Key ideas

- Attention: for queries $Q \in \mathbb R^{T \times d_k}$, keys $K \in \mathbb R^{T \times d_k}$, values $V \in \mathbb R^{T \times d_v}$, $$\mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V,$$ with the softmax over the key axis, row by row. The $\sqrt{d_k}$ keeps the logit variance at order one when $q$ and $k$ have unit-variance components.
- Multi-head: $H$ heads each with $d_k = d_v = d/H$, computed in parallel from projections $W_Q^h, W_K^h, W_V^h$, concatenated, and mixed by $W_O \in \mathbb R^{d \times d}$. Parameter count per attention layer is $4d^2$ (ignoring biases), independent of $H$.
- The block: $h \leftarrow h + \mathrm{MHA}(\mathrm{LN}(h))$, then $h \leftarrow h + \mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{LN}(h))$, with the MLP typically $d \to 4d \to d$, so $8d^2$ parameters. The residual stream of width $d$ is what every sublayer reads from and writes to.
- Cost in $T$: the score matrix $QK^\top$ costs $O(T^2 d)$ FLOPs and $O(H T^2)$ memory per layer if materialised; the projections and MLP cost $O(T d^2)$. The quadratic term dominates when $T \gtrsim d$.
- Causal mask: set score $(i, j)$ to $-\infty$ for $j > i$ before the softmax, so position $i$ attends only to positions $\le i$; this is what makes a decoder a valid autoregressive model and makes training on all positions in parallel equivalent to $T$ next-token predictions.
- Layer normalisation standardises across the feature dimension of a single token, so it has no batch dependence and no train/test discrepancy, unlike batch norm (week 8).
- Positional information must be injected separately; attention itself is permutation-equivariant over positions. Sinusoidal or learned absolute embeddings are the 2017 choice; rotary embeddings appear in week 11 and week 18.
- What attention replaced: recurrent networks process tokens sequentially and carry state through a bottleneck; attention gives every position direct access to every other at the cost of the quadratic term.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Attention FLOPs and memory vs sequence length; causal mask by hand

Part 1. Fix a decoder block with width $d$, $H$ heads, head dimension $d/H$, MLP hidden width $4d$, batch size 1, and sequence length $T$. Count, as exact leading-order expressions in $T$, $d$, $H$: (a) the FLOPs of the $Q, K, V$ projections; (b) the FLOPs of forming $QK^\top$ and of applying the attention weights to $V$; (c) the FLOPs of the output projection and the MLP; (d) the activation memory in elements needed for the backward pass if the attention weights are stored, and separately the $O(T^2)$ part. Count one multiply-add as two FLOPs and state that convention. Then give the $T$ at which the attention FLOPs equal the projection-plus-MLP FLOPs, as a function of $d$.

Part 2. For $T = 4$, write the $4 \times 4$ causal mask as a matrix of $0$ and $-\infty$ added to the scores. Take an explicit score matrix of your choice with small integer entries, apply the mask, compute the row softmax by hand, and confirm each row sums to one with zero weight on future positions. State what the mask does to the gradient with respect to masked scores.

## Common misconceptions

- "Attention is $O(T^2)$ in everything." → The score computation is quadratic in $T$; the projections and MLP are linear in $T$ and quadratic in $d$, and dominate at short context.
- "Layer norm and batch norm are interchangeable normalisers." → Layer norm normalises over features of one token with no batch statistics; batch norm normalises over the batch and has different train/test behaviour.
- "The causal mask is only needed at inference." → It is needed in training to make the parallel computation over positions match autoregressive prediction; without it the model sees the answer.
