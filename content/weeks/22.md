# Week 22: CS336: scaling laws

## Why this week

Week 16 read the scaling-law papers; this week reproduces the method on supplied data, which is the only way to know what the fit's error bars look like. The inference-aware optimum (Sardana and Frankle) is the version a founder needs: a model that will serve many tokens should be trained past the Chinchilla point. Week 25's budget reconstruction and the Gate 2 budget (week 27) rest on this.

## Objectives

- Given loss measurements on a grid of $(N, D)$ at several FLOP budgets, fit an IsoFLOP frontier and extract the compute-optimal $N^*(C)$ and $D^*(C)$ with a stated fitting procedure.
- Derive how a fixed lifetime inference budget shifts the optimal allocation toward more data and fewer parameters.
- Report the sensitivity of the fitted exponents to the choice of fit (which points, which functional form).

## Key ideas

- An IsoFLOP profile fixes $C$ and varies $N$ (so $D = C/6N$); loss against $\log N$ is roughly a parabola, and its minimum is the compute-optimal $N^*(C)$. Repeating over several $C$ and fitting $\log N^* $ against $\log C$ gives the allocation exponent.
- The parametric alternative fits $L(N,D) = E + A N^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta}$ to all points at once; the compute-optimal allocation follows from the first-order conditions derived in week 16.
- The inference-aware objective: total lifetime compute is $C_{\text{train}} + C_{\text{inf}} \approx 6ND + 2N D_{\text{inf}}$ with $D_{\text{inf}}$ the tokens served over the model's life. Minimizing loss for fixed total cost, or minimizing total cost for a target loss, shifts the optimum to smaller $N$ and larger $D$ as $D_{\text{inf}}$ grows.
- Fitted exponents have uncertainty that the papers rarely report; a bootstrap over the measured points gives a first estimate.
- Extrapolation: the frontier is trusted only inside the range of $C$ it was fitted on; the optimum at $10\times$ the largest budget is an extrapolation of a fit of minima.
- Data constraints break the picture: if $D^*$ exceeds the available unique tokens, repeated epochs are needed and the law's $D$ no longer means what it did.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Fit an IsoFLOP frontier on supplied data; inference-aware optimum

Part 1. You are given (from the CS336 assignment handout on scaling laws) a table of training runs with $(N, D, C, L)$. Group runs by $C$. For each group fit a quadratic in $\log N$ to $L$ and record the minimizer $N^*(C)$ and $D^*(C) = C/6N^*$. Fit $\log N^* = a + \alpha_C \log C$ and $\log D^* = b + \beta_C \log C$ by least squares. Report $\alpha_C$, $\beta_C$ and the implied tokens-per-parameter ratio as a function of $C$. Then refit using only the two largest budgets and report how much the exponents move.

Part 2. Fix a target loss $L_0$ reachable on your fitted frontier. Let the model serve $D_{\text{inf}}$ tokens over its life with cost $2N$ FLOPs per token. Using the parametric form $L(N,D)$ with exponents from Part 1 (or from Chinchilla's reported values, labelled as such), set up the problem of minimizing $6ND + 2N D_{\text{inf}}$ subject to $L(N,D) = L_0$. Derive the first-order conditions and state how $N^*$ and $D^*$ move as $D_{\text{inf}}$ goes from $0$ to $10^{3}$ times $D^*_{\text{Chinchilla}}$. Give the result as formulas and a qualitative statement; a plot from your own code is acceptable, numbers are not supplied here.

Assumptions to state: the $6ND$ and $2N$ cost models, the independence of the loss from batch size and schedule, and that $L_0$ lies inside the fitted range.

## Common misconceptions

- "Chinchilla-optimal is optimal." → It minimizes training compute for a loss; once inference is counted, smaller models trained longer are cheaper over the lifetime.
- "The exponents are properties of transformers." → They are fitted on a particular data distribution, tokenizer and schedule; change those and the exponents move.
- "A good fit means a good extrapolation." → The frontier is a fit of fitted minima; its uncertainty compounds, and nothing in the method bounds it outside the measured budgets.
