# Week 36: Constitutional AI, system cards, multimodal quick reads, SAEs

## Why this week

This is the alignment-and-safety landscape at lecture level: how a model is steered by model-written feedback (Constitutional AI), how the resulting behaviour is reported (a frontier system card), the two known failure modes of preference optimization (sycophancy, reward hacking), and a one-hour view of what interpretability currently offers. The system-card note is the first exercise in reading a document whose job is partly persuasion, which is the skill weeks 39 and 43 extend.

## Objectives

- Describe the Constitutional AI pipeline (critique, revision, AI-labelled preferences, RL) and state which step replaces human labels and which does not.
- Given a frontier system card, list each headline safety claim with the test method behind it and classify the method as reported, derived or absent.
- State what a sparse autoencoder is trained to do on residual-stream activations and what it does not establish about model behaviour.

## Key ideas

- Constitutional AI substitutes a written set of principles plus a model for human preference labels in the harmlessness objective; the helpfulness labels remain human. The reward model is then trained on AI-labelled comparisons.
- Sycophancy and reward hacking are both consequences of optimizing against a proxy of what is wanted; the proxy is the reward model or the human rater, and the failure appears as agreement with the user or exploitation of the rater's blind spots.
- A system card reports evaluations; the questions are the same six as for a paper, with "judge reliability" doing most of the work when the evaluation is model-graded.
- Contrastive multimodal models (CLIP) align image and text embeddings; Flamingo and LLaVA connect a vision encoder to a language model with a small adapter trained on paired data. Read for the objective and the data, not the architecture details.
- A sparse autoencoder decomposes activations into a sparse combination of learned directions; a direction that reads as a concept is a hypothesis about the model, not a verified mechanism.

## Common misconceptions

- "Constitutional AI removes humans from the loop." → It replaces human harmlessness labels with model labels under written principles; helpfulness labels and the principles themselves are human.
- "A system card is an evaluation." → It is a report of evaluations chosen by the developer; the reader's job is to recover the methods and the omissions.
- "An interpretable SAE feature explains a behaviour." → It names a direction correlated with inputs; a causal claim needs an intervention and a behavioural effect.
