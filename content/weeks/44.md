# Week 44: Oral defence

## Why this week

The final week tests whether the programme produced judgment rather than fluency: a one-hour adversarial defence of the capstone, a delayed closed-book retest of three derivations, the competency ledger finalised from unaided evidence only, and a one-page note on what to study next. The examiner is a working researcher if reachable, otherwise the maintainer of the canonical document.

## Objectives

- Defend the write-up for one hour against the question bank below, answering each question by saying what would differ if the claim were false.
- Redo three of the six closed-book derivations from memory, in correct notation, and record pass or fail for each.
- Finalise the competency ledger with every level justified by a closed-book answer or an audited artifact, and write the one-page what-next note.

## Key ideas

- The failure mode to watch for is fluent description without being able to say what would differ if the claim were false; every answer in the defence should contain a counterfactual.
- Adversarial means the examiner tries to break the result, not to understand it; prepare by attacking your own write-up with the six judgment questions first.
- A delayed retest measures retention, not learning; a level drops if the retest fails, as the checkpoints table states.
- Competency levels: U unassessed, 1 can explain with assistance, 2 solves a familiar problem alone, 3 transfers to an unfamiliar case and states the limits, 4 defends an original analysis under critique. A level is set only from a closed-book answer or an audited artifact, never from completing a resource.
- The ledger's Target column is what the one-year plan aimed for (2, with generative and multimodal at 1); the final ledger records where you are, not where you aimed.
- The what-next note names gaps by the depth map's "left out" row and by the retest results, not by interest.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

One hour, adversarial; delayed retest of three derivations; competency ledger finalized; one page on what to study next

Oral-defence question bank (the examiner picks; each question names the failure mode it probes):

1. State your result as an interval. What observation would have refuted it? (fluent description without a counterfactual)
2. Why is your baseline the strongest cheap alternative and not a straw man? (baseline strength)
3. Were baseline and treatment run under the same model and the same token or dollar budget? Show the log rows. (matched budgets)
4. How do you know the test set was not seen during pipeline iteration or model training? (contamination)
5. How many seeds or repeats, and how large is the between-seed spread relative to your effect? (seed variance)
6. If a judge model scored outputs, what was its agreement with your hand labels, and on how many items? (judge reliability)
7. What does your metric fail to capture about the task a user actually has? (construct validity)
8. Which hand-check discrepancy was largest, and what caused it? (auditing versus trusting the agent)
9. Which runs were reruns, and does the result hold on first attempts only? (selective reporting)
10. Name the alternative explanation you could not rule out. What experiment would rule it out? (unaddressed alternatives)
11. If the effect were real but half the size, what would you do differently? (decision relevance)
12. Reconstruct the cost per successful task from the log, by hand, now. (arithmetic under pressure)
13. Which protocol field did you change after seeing data, and why is the result still interpretable? (pre-registration)
14. Pick one number in your write-up and derive it from raw outputs. (traceability)
15. What would a team with ten times the budget do next, and what would still be unknowable? (limits)

Delayed retest: pick three of the six closed-book derivations and redo them from memory, in correct notation, within the hour's allotment agreed with the examiner: backprop through a transformer block; the ELBO; the policy gradient with baseline; training-step memory and FLOPs under tensor and data parallelism; DPO from the KL-regularized optimum plus Bradley-Terry; the GRPO estimator and its failure cases. Record pass or fail per derivation; a fail drops the matching competency level.

Competency-ledger finalisation: for each of the 12 rows, cite the closed-book answer or audited artifact that justifies the level and its date; rows with no such evidence stay at U or at the last retested level; set retest dates for any level changed this week.

What-to-study-next note, one page, headings: retest failures; competencies below target and why; depth-map "left out" areas now relevant to your work; one paper slot per month, with the selection rule; what the examiner attacked that you could not answer.

## Self-check

1. For your primary result, state in one sentence what would differ in the data if the claim were false.
2. Which three derivations did you retest, and which steps did you have to reconstruct rather than recall?
3. For each competency row at level 2 or above, name the unaided evidence that set it.

## Common misconceptions

- "Completing all 49 weeks sets the competency levels." Correct: completing a resource counts for nothing in the ledger; only closed-book answers and audited artifacts set a level.
- "The defence tests the result." Correct: it tests method and judgment; a null result defended with counterfactuals passes, a positive result described fluently without them does not.
- "A failed retest means the week was wasted." Correct: it means the level drops and the derivation goes on the what-next note; retention is what the delayed retest measures.
