# Week 12: Prince ch. 14, 17: unsupervised learning, VAEs

## Why this week

The ELBO is one of the six closed-book derivations and is tested at [week 17](/week/17). It is the maximum-likelihood recipe of [week 5](/week/5) applied to a model with a latent variable whose marginal likelihood is intractable, and the bound it produces is the template for the diffusion loss of [week 13](/week/13) and for every KL-regularised objective in Phase 3 ([week 28](/week/28), [week 30](/week/30)).

## Objectives

- Derive the evidence lower bound from the marginal log-likelihood in two independent ways (Jensen, and the KL identity) and state exactly when it is tight.
- Write the VAE's ELBO under a Gaussian encoder and prior in closed form for the KL term and explain the reparametrisation trick as a change of variables for a gradient estimator.
- Place the VAE among unsupervised objectives (density estimation, autoencoding, contrastive) by what each one maximises.

## Key ideas

- A latent-variable model is $p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x \mid z)\, p(z)\, dz$; the integral is intractable for a neural decoder, so the likelihood cannot be maximised directly.
- Introduce any density $q(z)$ with the right support. The identity to derive is $$\log p_\theta(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb E_{q(z)}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\|\,p(z)\big)}_{\mathcal L(\theta, q;\, x)} + \mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\|\,p_\theta(z \mid x)\big),$$ so $\mathcal L \le \log p_\theta(x)$ with equality iff $q = p_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$.
- The VAE chooses $q = q_\phi(z \mid x)$, an encoder network, and maximises $\mathcal L$ jointly in $\theta$ and $\phi$: the first term is reconstruction, the second regularises the posterior toward the prior.
- With $p(z) = \mathcal N(0, I)$ and $q_\phi(z \mid x) = \mathcal N(\mu, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2))$, the KL term is $\tfrac12 \sum_j (\mu_j^2 + \sigma_j^2 - 1 - \log \sigma_j^2)$, in closed form.
- Reparametrisation: write $z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$ so the expectation is over a fixed distribution and the gradient with respect to $\phi$ passes through $\mu$ and $\sigma$; the score-function estimator would also be unbiased but has much higher variance.
- The gap $\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z \mid x) \| p_\theta(z \mid x))$ is the price of the variational family; a Gaussian encoder cannot match a multimodal true posterior, and the bound is loose there.
- Unsupervised learning compared by objective: density estimation maximises $\log p(x)$ or a bound on it; autoencoders minimise reconstruction error with a bottleneck and need not define a density; contrastive methods (week 13) learn a representation by discriminating positives from negatives.
- Posterior collapse: with a powerful decoder the optimum may set $q_\phi(z \mid x) = p(z)$ and ignore $z$; the ELBO is then the decoder's own likelihood and the latent carries nothing.

Derive the ELBO before reading the derivation

Before opening Prince ch. 17 or the paper, with notes closed: let $p_\theta(x, z) = p_\theta(x \mid z) p(z)$ with $z$ continuous and $p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x, z)\, dz$ intractable. Let $q(z)$ be any density with $q(z) > 0$ wherever $p_\theta(z \mid x) > 0$.

(a) Starting from $\log p_\theta(x)$, derive a lower bound $\mathcal L(q)$ that involves only $p_\theta(x \mid z)$, $p(z)$ and $q(z)$, and state the inequality used and its condition for equality.

(b) Derive the same bound a second way by writing $\log p_\theta(x) - \mathcal L(q)$ as a single KL divergence, and state which two distributions it compares.

(c) Specialise to $q(z) = q_\phi(z \mid x)$ and write the two terms of the ELBO as a reconstruction term and a regularisation term, giving the sign of each.

(d) For $p(z) = \mathcal N(0, I_d)$ and $q_\phi(z \mid x) = \mathcal N(\mu, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2))$, write the regularisation term in closed form (you may derive it or state it; say which).

(e) State why $\nabla_\phi\, \mathbb E_{q_\phi(z \mid x)}[f(z)]$ cannot be estimated by sampling $z$ and differentiating $f$, and give the reparametrised estimator that fixes this.

## Common misconceptions

- "The ELBO is the VAE's loss." → The ELBO is a bound on the log-likelihood for any $q$; the VAE is one choice of $q$ (an amortised encoder) and one way of estimating the gradient.
- "The KL term pulls the latent toward zero to compress it." → It pulls the approximate posterior toward the prior; the reconstruction term pulls the other way, and the balance is set by the likelihood model, not by a compression argument.
- "Reparametrisation is needed because the expectation is intractable." → The expectation is estimated by Monte Carlo either way; reparametrisation is about obtaining a low-variance gradient with respect to the parameters of the sampling distribution.
