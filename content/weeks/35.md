# Week 35: DeepSeek-R1, DAPO, Dr. GRPO, Yue et al.

## Why this week

The four readings are the 2025 argument about what RLVR does: R1 is the headline result, DAPO and Dr. GRPO are corrections to the estimator's failure cases, and Yue et al. ask whether the gains are new capability or sharpened sampling. The two notes written here are the first drafts of judgments you will defend in the week-40 oral and in the capstone, and the question is one the curriculum tracks as unresolved.

## Objectives

- State, from the GRPO formula of week 34, exactly why an all-correct or all-wrong group contributes no signal and what DAPO and Dr. GRPO each change about it.
- Give the mechanism of length bias in per-token-normalized GRPO and the metric that would reveal it in a training log.
- Write a two-page note that states a falsifiable criterion separating "RLVR adds capability" from "RLVR sharpens sampling" and says what each paper's evidence does to it.

## Key ideas

- Zero-variance groups: with binary rewards and $k \in \{0, G\}$, $s = 0$ and $\hat A_i$ is undefined or zero; those prompts produce no gradient, so the effective batch shrinks as the policy improves on easy prompts and never learns from prompts it always fails.
- Length bias: averaging the per-token loss over $|o_i|$ gives each token in a long completion a smaller weight; whether long wrong answers are penalized less, and long right answers rewarded less, depends on the normalization, and the fixes change the normalization rather than the reward.
- DAPO and Dr. GRPO change the normalization and the sampling of groups (which prompts are kept, how the standard deviation is used, how tokens are weighted); read them as alternative estimators of the same gradient.
- Capability versus sharpening: if RLVR only reallocates probability mass toward answers the base model could already produce, pass@$k$ for large $k$ should not rise, and may fall, while pass@1 rises. Yue et al. measure exactly this; the base-model pass@$k$ at large $k$ is the baseline.
- The claim has to be posed per task family and per $k$; one number cannot settle it.

## Common misconceptions

- "GRPO learns from every prompt in the batch." → Prompts whose group has constant reward contribute nothing; the effective batch is smaller than the nominal one.
- "pass@1 improved, so the model can solve new problems." → pass@1 can rise through reallocation alone; new capability requires a problem solved at some $k$ that the base model does not solve at any comparable $k$.
- "Length bias is a reward problem." → It enters through the token normalization in the estimator; the fixes in DAPO and Dr. GRPO change the normalization, not the verifier.
