# Week 28: RLHF Book: definitions, problem setup, preference data, reward modeling

## Why this week

Every post-training method from here to [week 35](/week/35) is a way of optimising one objective: expected reward under a KL penalty to a reference policy. This week fixes that objective and the Bradley-Terry model of preference data from which the reward is learned; [week 30](/week/30) derives DPO from exactly these two ingredients and [week 34](/week/34) builds the GRPO estimator on the same objective.

## Objectives

- Write the KL-regularised RLHF objective from memory, naming each term and its role.
- Derive the Bradley-Terry likelihood of a preference pair and the resulting reward-model loss, stating what the reward is identified up to.
- Explain, in one paragraph, how a chat-template mismatch between training and inference corrupts a fine-tune without any error being raised.

## Key ideas

- Setup: prompts $x \sim \mathcal{D}$, policy $\pi_\theta(y \mid x)$, reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ (the SFT model), reward $r(x, y)$. The objective is
  $$\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D},\, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(x,y)\big] - \beta\, \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}\big[\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big)\big].$$
- $\beta$ trades reward against drift from the reference; the KL term is what keeps a learned, imperfect reward from being exploited far from the data that trained it.
- Preference data: for a prompt $x$, two completions $y_w \succ y_l$. The Bradley-Terry model sets $P(y_w \succ y_l \mid x) = \sigma\big(r(x,y_w) - r(x,y_l)\big)$ with $\sigma$ the logistic function.
- Maximum likelihood on pairs gives the reward-model loss $-\mathbb{E}\big[\log \sigma(r_\phi(x,y_w) - r_\phi(x,y_l))\big]$; the reward is identified only up to an additive per-prompt constant, since the difference is all the data constrain.
- The reward model is a classifier trained on a finite preference set; its errors off-distribution are the reason the KL term exists, and its calibration on held-out pairs (agreement rate) is the quantity reports should show.
- Chat templates are the serialisation of roles and turns into tokens; the model has only ever seen one serialisation, so a different template at inference is a distribution shift at every position.
- Post-training datasets (Labonne §3): instruction pairs, multi-turn chat, synthetic data generated by a stronger model, quality filtering by a judge model. Each is a design choice with a construct-validity question attached.

## Common misconceptions

- "The reward model outputs a calibrated score of quality" → It outputs a quantity whose per-prompt differences are trained to fit preference pairs; its scale and offset are arbitrary.
- "The KL term is a regulariser like weight decay" → It is a constraint against exploiting reward-model error off the preference distribution; removing it changes what the optimum is, not only how fast you reach it.
- "If the fine-tuned model generates fluent text, the template is right" → Fluency survives template mismatch; turn boundaries, stop behaviour and instruction following are what break.
