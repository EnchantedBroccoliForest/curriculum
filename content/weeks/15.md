# Week 15: Sutton and Barto ch. 13; Spinning Up policy-gradient pages

## Why this week

The policy gradient theorem is the single result that every post-training algorithm in weeks 28–35 instantiates: PPO is a clipped surrogate of it, GRPO is a particular baseline for it, and DPO is what you get when you solve its KL-regularized version in closed form. The baseline argument is also the clearest example in the plan of a variance-reduction claim that can be checked by calculation rather than by experiment. It is one of the three Gate 1 derivations in week 17.

## Objectives

- Derive the policy gradient theorem for the episodic case from the definition of the objective, keeping track of where the log-derivative trick and the Markov property are used.
- Show that subtracting a state-dependent baseline leaves the gradient estimator unbiased, and state the condition under which it reduces variance.
- Map each term of Spinning Up's PPO implementation (advantage estimate, ratio, clip, value loss, entropy bonus) to the corresponding symbol in the equations.

## Key ideas

- Objective: $J(\theta) = \mathbb E_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]$ with $R(\tau)=\sum_t \gamma^t r_t$. The gradient cannot be moved inside the expectation directly because the sampling distribution depends on $\theta$; the log-derivative identity $\nabla_\theta p_\theta = p_\theta \nabla_\theta \log p_\theta$ fixes that.
- The policy gradient theorem, in the form to be derived:
$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\,\big(G_t - b(s_t)\big)\Big],$$
where $G_t$ is the reward-to-go and $b$ is any function of the state only. The environment dynamics $p(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)$ drop out because they do not depend on $\theta$.
- Reward-to-go: replacing $R(\tau)$ by $G_t$ uses causality (actions cannot affect earlier rewards). It is a variance reduction, not an approximation.
- Baseline: $\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)}[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a\mid s)] = 0$ is why $b(s)$ does not bias the estimator. Choosing $b(s) = v_\pi(s)$ turns the weight into the advantage $A(s,a) = q(s,a) - v(s)$.
- REINFORCE is the Monte Carlo estimator of the theorem; actor–critic replaces $G_t$ by a bootstrapped estimate and trades variance for bias. Generalized advantage estimation interpolates with a parameter $\lambda$.
- PPO's objective replaces $\log\pi_\theta$ by the probability ratio $\rho_t = \pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t\mid s_t)$ and clips it: $L^{\text{CLIP}} = \mathbb E[\min(\rho_t \hat A_t,\ \text{clip}(\rho_t, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat A_t)]$. The ratio makes several gradient steps per batch valid as importance-weighted estimates; the clip stops the ratio from drifting.
- Everything above is on-policy: the samples must come from the current (or recent) policy. This is why RLHF generates fresh completions every step and why that is expensive.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Policy gradient theorem; baseline variance-reduction argument; map Spinning Up's PPO code to the equations

Part 1 (policy gradient theorem). Setting: finite-horizon episodic MDP, horizon $T$, stochastic policy $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ differentiable in $\theta$, trajectory density $p_\theta(\tau) = \mu(s_0)\prod_t \pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,p(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)$. Derive $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$ as an expectation over trajectories in the reward-to-go form displayed above (with $b \equiv 0$). State explicitly where you use (a) the log-derivative identity, (b) the independence of the dynamics from $\theta$, (c) causality.

Part 2 (baseline). Let $b: \mathcal S \to \mathbb R$ be any bounded function. Prove that adding $-b(s_t)$ to the weight leaves the expectation of the estimator unchanged. Then, for a single state and a single-step problem, write the variance of the estimator as a function of $b$ and derive the minimizing $b^*$. State whether $b^* = v_\pi(s)$, and if not, what it is and why $v_\pi$ is used in practice anyway.

Part 3 (code mapping). Open Spinning Up's PPO implementation. Produce a two-column table: left, each quantity in $L^{\text{CLIP}}$, the value loss and the advantage estimate; right, the variable or line that computes it. Note every place where the code's estimate differs from the equation (bootstrapping at episode cut-off, advantage normalization, the number of epochs per batch).

## Common misconceptions

- "The baseline reduces variance by subtracting the mean reward." → The baseline is a state-dependent control variate; the optimal one is a gradient-norm-weighted average of returns, not the plain mean, and any state-only baseline is unbiased.
- "PPO constrains the KL divergence to the old policy." → The clipped surrogate bounds the per-sample ratio; the KL-penalty variant is a different algorithm, and the clip does not guarantee a KL bound.
- "Policy gradient methods are off-policy because they reuse a batch for several epochs." → The ratio makes a few steps near $\theta_{\text{old}}$ approximately valid; the samples still come from a recent policy, and reusing old data further is where the method breaks.
