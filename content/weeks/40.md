# Week 40: Gate 3 and oral exam

## Why this week

Gate 3 closes Phase 3 with the last two of the six closed-book derivations and a one-hour adversarial oral on anything from Phases 0–3. Competency rows 8–11 (post-training objectives and reward design; reasoning and test-time compute; retrieval, agents, evaluation, safety; and the row set at Gate 2 that is retested here if chosen) are set from this week's unaided work alone. Nothing from a completed resource counts.

## Objectives

- Derive DPO from the KL-regularized optimum plus Bradley-Terry, closed-book, with the coverage assumption stated.
- Derive the GRPO group-relative advantage estimator and its failure cases, closed-book, in the notation of week 34.
- Answer one hour of adversarial questions on Phases 0–3, saying for each claim what would differ if it were false.

## Key ideas

- Closed-book means: no notes, no model, no search; blank paper and a timer. Record the date, the time taken and any step you could not complete; that record is the evidence for the competency ledger.
- DPO's chain: the KL-regularized objective $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[r] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\text{ref}})$ has a closed-form optimum in terms of $r$ and $\pi_{\text{ref}}$; invert it to express $r$ through $\pi$; substitute into the Bradley-Terry likelihood $\sigma(r(y_w) - r(y_l))$; the partition function cancels. The coverage assumption is where the derivation can fail silently.
- GRPO's chain: policy gradient with baseline; the group mean as the baseline estimate; the standard deviation as a per-prompt rescaling; the per-token normalization; failure at $s = 0$ and length dependence.
- The oral's failure mode, from the curriculum: fluent description without being able to say what would differ if the claim were false. Every answer should end with the observation that would refute it.
- Level 2 on a competency row means a familiar problem solved alone; level 3 requires transfer to an unfamiliar case with its limits stated. The oral is where level 3 can be demonstrated.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Closed-book: DPO; the GRPO estimator. One-hour adversarial oral. Competency rows 8–11 set

Derivation 1 (DPO). Given a reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, a reward $r(x, y)$, a temperature $\beta > 0$, and preference pairs $(x, y_w, y_l)$ with Bradley-Terry likelihood $p(y_w \succ y_l \mid x) = \sigma\big(r(x, y_w) - r(x, y_l)\big)$: derive the optimal policy of the KL-regularized objective, express $r$ in terms of $\pi$ and $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, and write the resulting maximum-likelihood objective in $\pi_\theta$ alone. State the assumption on the support of $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ relative to the preference data that the derivation needs, and what breaks without it.

Derivation 2 (GRPO estimator). In the notation of week 34, derive $\hat A_i = (r_i - \bar r)/s$ from the policy gradient with baseline, write the per-token clipped surrogate with KL penalty, and state the failure cases: zero-variance groups and the length dependence of the per-token average.

Oral, one hour, adversarial. Question bank, to be drawn from without warning (the examiner may add their own):

1. Backprop is reverse-mode AD: what does forward mode compute, and when is it cheaper?
2. Derive cross-entropy from maximum likelihood; where does the softmax come from?
3. State Adam's invariance precisely and name one rescaling it is not invariant to.
4. Attention FLOPs and memory as functions of sequence length; which bound binds on one GPU at 8k context?
5. Write the ELBO and say which gap it leaves open.
6. Policy gradient with baseline: why is the baseline free, and when is it not?
7. Chinchilla: what does the power-law fit assume, and what observation would break it?
8. Training-step memory under tensor and data parallelism for a 7B model on 64 GPUs; which term dominates.
9. Speculative decoding: expected speedup as a function of acceptance rate; what makes acceptance fall.
10. RLVR: does it add capability or sharpen sampling? Name the measurement that decides it.
11. Your week-38 agent: which input was untrusted, and what would an injection there have done?
12. For any benchmark number you cite: which of the six judgment questions does it fail?

Record, per question: answered unaided, answered with prompting, or not answered. Competency rows 8–11 are set from this record and the two derivations.

## Common misconceptions

- "A derivation reproduced from notes the day before counts." → Closed-book is the only evidence the ledger accepts; the retest at week 44 exists to catch recall that did not persist.
- "Passing the derivations sets the competency rows." → The rows are set from the derivations and the oral record together; a derivation done with prompting is recorded as such.
- "The oral tests recall." → It tests whether you can say what would differ if a claim were false; description without a falsifier scores as not answered.
