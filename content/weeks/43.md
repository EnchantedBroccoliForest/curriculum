# Week 43: Write-up, four to six pages

## Why this week

The write-up turns the audit log into an argument that another person can check. Its job is to report what the protocol produced, with uncertainty, the explanations that compete with yours, and the tests that failed, so that the week-44 defence has something concrete to attack. The failure mode to avoid is fluent description that never says what would differ if the claim were false.

## Objectives

- Write four to six pages following the outline below, with every number traceable to a row of the audit log.
- Report the primary result as an interval on paired differences, with the registered falsifier stated next to it.
- List at least two alternative explanations and every test that failed, including deviations from the protocol.

## Key ideas

- The primary comparison is the one registered in week 41; everything else is exploratory and labelled as such.
- Uncertainty comes from paired differences across units and across seeds where used; a point estimate without an interval is not a result.
- Alternative explanations are the six judgment questions turned on yourself: contamination, baseline strength, matched budgets, seed variance, judge reliability, construct validity.
- Failed tests are part of the result; a write-up with no failed tests has either not tested enough or has omitted them.
- State what would change the conclusion: the observation that would make you reverse it, and whether the study could have produced it.
- For an analytical capstone (report audit or cost analysis), "results" are the reconstructed numbers labelled reported, derived, or unknown, and uncertainty is the range implied by the unknowns.
- Figures carry the paired differences and their interval; tables carry the audit-log totals (runs, reruns, cost, hand-check discrepancy).

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Results with uncertainty; alternative explanations; failed tests

Write-up outline with target lengths (four to six pages in total):

1. **Question and falsifier** (quarter page): the registered question, the claim, and the refuting observation, verbatim from the week-41 protocol.
2. **Protocol as run** (half to one page): unit of analysis, splits, metric, baseline, treatment, stopping rule, budget; then every deviation from the registered protocol with its reason.
3. **Results with uncertainty** (one to one and a half pages): the primary paired comparison with its interval and method; seeds or repeats; hand-check discrepancy rate; cost per run and per successful task; exploratory results separately and labelled.
4. **Alternative explanations** (one page): each of the six judgment questions applied to this result, with the evidence for and against, and the ones you cannot rule out.
5. **Failed tests** (half page): what was tried and did not work, including runs discarded and why, and metric or judge checks that failed.
6. **What would change the conclusion** (quarter page): the observation or re-analysis that would reverse it, and whether this study could have produced it.
7. **Limits** (quarter to half page): what the result does not establish; the population it does not cover; what a team with more budget would do next.

<summary>Hints</summary>

- Draft section 4 before section 3; knowing which alternative explanations you must address determines which numbers section 3 has to report.
- Every number in the text should have a cell in the audit log or a line in the analysis script that produces it.
- Four pages that answer the question beat six that describe the work.

## Self-check

1. Without opening the write-up, state the primary result as an interval and the falsifier. Does the interval exclude it, include it, or straddle it?
2. Which of the six judgment questions is the weakest point of your study, and what in the write-up says so?
3. Name one failed test you reported. What did it rule out?

## Common misconceptions

- "A negative or null result is a failed capstone." Correct: the capstone demonstrates method; a null result with a tight interval and honest alternatives is a complete demonstration.
- "Alternative explanations weaken the write-up." Correct: unaddressed alternatives are what a reader finds first; addressing them is the substance of the argument.
- "Longer is more thorough." Correct: the length bound exists so that every section carries information; padding hides the result.
