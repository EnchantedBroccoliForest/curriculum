# Week 4: Experimental judgment: leakage, baselines, judges, contamination

## Why this week

From this week on, every paper read in the programme is put through six standing questions: contamination, baseline strength, matched budgets, seed variance, judge reliability, construct validity. The week supplies the three failure modes behind them from the primary sources, and closes Phase 0 with Gate 0 and the first pace review, which decides whether the hour budget holds.

## Objectives

- Write an experimental protocol for comparing two models that states the unit of analysis, the paired design and how the uncertainty of the difference is estimated.
- Classify a leakage case from your own quantitative work by the taxonomy in Kapoor and Narayanan and state the remedy.
- Explain the nanochat training script line by line, naming what each block of code does in the vocabulary of [weeks 1–3](/week/1).

## Key ideas

- **Leakage**: information from the evaluation set reaching training, through duplicate records, preprocessing fit on all data, features that are proxies for the label, or temporal ordering violated by a random split. Kapoor and Narayanan's taxonomy and model-info sheet are the checklist; the symptom is test performance that does not transfer.
- **Temporal leakage** in a time series: any feature computed with information from after the prediction time, including a normalisation statistic or a model selection step fit on the whole sample. The fix is a strictly forward-looking pipeline with an untouched later period.
- **Paired comparison**: evaluating two systems on the same items and testing the per-item differences, which removes item difficulty variance; report the mean difference with a bootstrap or paired-test interval over items, and over seeds where training is stochastic.
- **Baseline strength and matched budgets**: a gain over a weak or under-tuned baseline, or under unequal compute, data or tuning effort, is not evidence for the method. Ask what the baseline would have scored given the same budget.
- **LLM-as-a-judge** (Zheng et al.): a model scoring model outputs; biases documented there include position bias, verbosity bias and self-enhancement bias, with agreement to human raters reported as the reliability figure. A judge score is a measurement with its own error, to be validated against human labels on a sample.
- **Benchmark contamination**: test items present in pretraining data; detection by n-gram overlap, canary strings or perplexity gaps, none of which is conclusive. A reported score on a public benchmark is an upper bound on held-out ability.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Specify a paired comparison with uncertainty; one temporal-leakage case from quant work; annotate nanochat's training script. Pace review. Gate 0

1. **Paired comparison.** Specify, in one page, a protocol for deciding whether model A beats model B on a task of your choice: unit of analysis, the item set and how it was held out, the metric per item, the paired difference statistic, the resampling or test used for its uncertainty, the number of seeds if training is involved, the stopping rule, and the pre-registered threshold for "A is better". State which of the six judgment questions the protocol answers and which it cannot.
2. **Temporal-leakage case.** Write up one case from your own quantitative work (or a plausible reconstruction) in which future information entered a backtest or model: where it entered, how it was or could have been detected, what the corrected result looked like, and which category in Kapoor and Narayanan's taxonomy it falls under.
3. **Annotate nanochat's training script.** Read the training script line by line and add a comment to every block: data loading, model construction, optimizer setup, the step (forward, loss, backward, update, zero), logging, checkpointing, and any mixed-precision or distributed-training code. Mark lines you cannot yet explain; they are the reading list for Phase 1 and 2.
4. **Pace review.** Record measured hours for weeks 1–4 against the planned 24. Compute the mean and state whether the plan's 6 hours per week holds; if not, note by how much.
5. **Gate 0.** Confirm the Phase 0 checkpoint artifacts exist: micrograd with gradient check, the one-page AD derivation, the annotated training script.

## Common misconceptions

- A random train/test split protects against leakage → for time-ordered data it guarantees leakage; only a forward split with features computed from the past does.
- Agreement between an LLM judge and humans of 80% means the judge is reliable → reliability depends on the comparison; the inter-human agreement rate and the direction of disagreements matter as much as the figure.
- Contamination checks by n-gram overlap settle the question → they bound one kind of contamination; paraphrased or translated items evade them.
