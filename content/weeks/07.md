# Week 7: Prince ch. 8–9: performance, regularization

## Key ideas

- Classical decomposition for a fixed $x$: $\mathbb E[(y - \hat f(x))^2] = \text{noise} + \text{bias}^2 + \text{variance}$, with variance over training sets. It predicts a U-shaped test error in model capacity.
- Double descent (Nakkiran 2019): test error can rise near the interpolation threshold (where parameters roughly equal examples) and fall again beyond it; the effect appears in model size, in training epochs, and in sample size. The classical curve is a special case, not the rule.
- The minimum-norm interpolant: among all parameters that fit the data exactly, gradient descent from a small initialisation on a linear model converges to the one of minimum $\ell_2$ norm; this is the simplest formal instance of implicit regularisation.
- Grokking (Power 2022): on small algorithmic datasets, training accuracy reaches 100% long before validation accuracy, which later jumps to 100% after many more steps; weight decay shortens the delay. The phenomenon is a counterexample to "validation plateau means stop."
- Dropout (2014): each unit is zeroed with probability $p$ during training and the layer is rescaled at test time (or during training, in the inverted form); the paper's framing is an ensemble over $2^n$ subnetworks sharing weights.
- Explicit regularisers (L2, weight decay, early stopping, data augmentation, label smoothing) are additive terms or procedural changes; implicit regularisers are properties of the optimiser's path. Weight decay and L2 coincide for SGD but not for Adam.
- SGD noise scale: with learning rate $\alpha$ and batch size $B$, the per-step gradient noise has covariance proportional to $\alpha^2 / B$ times the per-example gradient covariance; flat minima are favoured relative to sharp ones under such noise.

## Common misconceptions

- "Overparametrised models overfit." → Models well past the interpolation threshold often generalise better than those near it; the classical U-curve describes one regime of capacity, not all of them.
- "Weight decay and L2 regularisation are the same thing." → They coincide for plain SGD; under Adam the L2 gradient is rescaled by the adaptive denominator, which is why decoupled weight decay (AdamW) exists.
- "Grokking shows the model was memorising until it suddenly understood." → The observation is a delayed generalisation under continued training with regularisation; the mechanism is debated, and "understanding" is not an operational quantity in the paper.
