# Week 30: RLHF Book: direct alignment

## Why this week

DPO is the fifth closed-book derivation and the one that ties Phase 3 together: it shows that the KL-regularised optimum has a closed form, and that a Bradley-Terry reward can be re-expressed in terms of the policy itself, removing the reward model from training. [Week 40](/week/40) tests it closed-book; [week 35](/week/35) and the practitioner comparison of DPO variants depend on knowing exactly what DPO assumes.

## Objectives

- State the coverage (support) assumption DPO relies on and what goes wrong empirically when it fails.

## Key ideas

- Inputs to the derivation. The KL-regularised objective from [week 28](/week/28):
  $$\max_\pi\; \mathbb{E}_{x,\, y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(x,y)\big] - \beta\, \mathbb{E}_x\big[\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big)\big],$$
  and the Bradley-Terry model of a preference pair:
  $$P(y_w \succ y_l \mid x) = \sigma\big(r(x,y_w) - r(x,y_l)\big).$$
- The objective is a KL-constrained maximisation over a distribution on completions for each fixed $x$; it has a unique maximiser expressible through $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, $r$, $\beta$ and a per-prompt normaliser $Z(x)$.
- Inverting that maximiser expresses $r$ as a function of $\pi$, $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ and $Z(x)$; in a Bradley-Terry difference the $Z(x)$ term cancels, which is what makes the reward model unnecessary.
- The resulting loss is maximum likelihood over preference pairs with the implicit reward $\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)}$; $\beta$ now scales the implicit reward rather than a separate penalty.
- Coverage: the derivation treats the reward as defined wherever the policy puts mass, but the preference data only constrain it on pairs sampled from (near) $\pi_{\text{ref}}$; off-support, the implicit reward is unconstrained and the policy can drift there. The IPO and KTO variants change the loss shape to address related failures; the comparison in the HF post is lecture-level.
- Observable metrics: the margin $\beta(\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w \mid x)} - \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l \mid x)})$, its sign accuracy, and the two log-probability terms separately. A margin that grows because both log-probabilities fall is a different regime from one where the chosen term rises.

## Common misconceptions

- "DPO has no reward model" → It has an implicit reward, $\beta \log(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})$, trained by the same Bradley-Terry likelihood; what is removed is a separate network and the sampling loop.
- "A rising margin means the model is improving" → The margin can rise while the chosen completion's probability falls; the two log-probability terms must be read separately.
- "$\beta$ is a learning rate" → It is the KL coefficient of the original objective and sets the scale of the implicit reward; it changes the optimum, not just the step size.
