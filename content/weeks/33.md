# Week 33: Raschka ch. 1–3 and inference-time scaling

## Why this week

Inference-time scaling is the other axis of capability after training compute: sampling more, searching, or verifying at test time. Raschka's opening chapters set up the evaluation harness you will run against; s1 and self-consistency are the two cleanest mechanisms; the Hugging Face post is a claim to audit, not a result to accept. The expected-gain calculation here is what makes the week-34 GRPO derivation and the week-35 "capability or sharpening" note precise.

## Objectives

- Run Raschka's evaluation notebook end to end and state what metric it computes, over which prompts, with which decoding settings.
- State the expected accuracy of best-of-$n$ with a verifier as an explicit function of $n$ and the verifier's error rates, and say what happens as $n \to \infty$.
- Produce a one-page audit of the "3B beats 70B on MATH-500" claim with the matched-budgets question answered first.

## Key ideas

- Self-consistency samples $n$ chains and takes the majority answer: no verifier, gain bounded by the mode of the answer distribution. Best-of-$n$ with a verifier keeps an accepted sample: gain depends on $p$ and the verifier's false-positive rate, which caps accuracy below 1 regardless of $n$.
- Test-time compute and training compute trade off; a fair comparison fixes total FLOPs per problem (or dollars) and reports accuracy at matched cost. The HF post's headline compares models of different sizes at different sampling budgets; the matched-budget curve is the thing to find.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Run the evaluation notebook; expected gain of best-of-n with a verifier as a function of n and verifier error; put the HF 3B-versus-70B claim through the six judgment questions, matched budgets first

Part 1. Run Raschka's evaluation notebook on a laptop. Record the model, the benchmark subset, the number of problems, the decoding temperature, and the accuracy with its binomial standard error.

Part 2. Assume $n$ i.i.d. samples, each correct with probability $p$. A verifier accepts a correct sample with probability $1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fn}}$ and accepts an incorrect sample with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fp}}$, independently across samples. Selection rule: return a uniformly random accepted sample if any sample is accepted, otherwise a uniformly random sample. Derive

$$
P_n(\text{correct}) \;=\; f\!\left(n,\, p,\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{fp}},\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{fn}}\right),
$$

then compute $\lim_{n\to\infty} P_n$ and the gain $P_n - p$ as a function of $n$. State the condition on $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fp}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fn}}$ and $p$ under which the gain is positive for all $n \ge 2$.

Part 3. Put the HF "3B beating 70B on MATH-500" claim through the six judgment questions, matched budgets first: at what test-time budget (tokens or FLOPs per problem) is the 3B model's accuracy reported, and what is the 70B model's accuracy at the same budget?

## Common misconceptions

- "3B with search beating 70B shows 70B is unnecessary." → A trade-off at one budget; the relevant comparison is 70B with the same search budget.
- "More samples always help." → With an imperfect verifier the accuracy saturates at a value set by the error rates; with majority voting it saturates at the mode of the answer distribution, which can be wrong.
