# Week 5: Prince ch. 3–5: shallow and deep networks, loss functions

## Why this week

Every loss in the rest of the programme is a negative log-likelihood under some conditional model, and every architecture is a parametrised family of functions whose expressiveness is the question of ch. 3–4. Fixing this now means the ELBO in [week 12](/week/12), the Bradley-Terry loss in week 28, and the DPO objective in week 30 read as instances of one recipe rather than new objects. The universal-approximation argument is the one place the plan asks for a sketch of why width suffices, which frames the depth-versus-width question of weeks 6–8.

## Objectives

- State the maximum-likelihood recipe (choose a conditional distribution $p(y \mid f_\theta(x))$, write $-\log$ of it, average over data) and apply it to Gaussian and categorical outputs without notes.
- Write a shallow network as $f(x) = \phi_0 + \sum_k \phi_k\, a(\theta_{k0} + \theta_k^\top x)$ and count its parameters and linear regions for 1-D input.
- Give the one-paragraph universal-approximation argument for a single hidden layer with a non-polynomial activation, naming what it does and does not say about depth.

## Key ideas

- Supervised learning as conditional density estimation: the network outputs the parameters of $p(y \mid x)$, not $y$. Regression with fixed variance gives $p(y \mid x) = \mathcal N(y; f_\theta(x), \sigma^2)$; classification gives a categorical with logits $f_\theta(x)$.
- The loss is the average negative log-likelihood, $L(\theta) = -\frac1N \sum_i \log p(y_i \mid f_\theta(x_i))$; maximising likelihood and minimising cross-entropy between the empirical and model distributions are the same problem.
- Under the Gaussian model, $$-\log \mathcal N(y; \mu, \sigma^2) = \frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \tfrac12 \log(2\pi\sigma^2),$$ so MSE is the likelihood with $\sigma$ held fixed; a learned $\sigma(x)$ is heteroscedastic regression and changes the gradient weighting.
- Under the categorical model with softmax $p_k = e^{z_k}/\sum_j e^{z_j}$, the loss is $-z_{y} + \log\sum_j e^{z_j}$; its gradient with respect to the logits is $p - e_y$, which is why log-sum-exp (primer P3) appears everywhere.
- A shallow network with ReLU activations is piecewise linear; with $D$ hidden units on 1-D input it has at most $D+1$ linear regions, and the number of regions grows polynomially in width but exponentially in depth for the same parameter count (Prince ch. 4).
- Composition: a deep network is $f_K \circ \cdots \circ f_1$, each layer an affine map followed by an elementwise nonlinearity; the hidden representation $h_\ell$ is what later layers see, and its dimension is a design choice independent of input and output dimension.
- Universal approximation is an existence statement about width at fixed depth 1 on a compact set; it says nothing about the number of units required, about learnability by gradient descent, or about generalisation.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Derive MSE and cross-entropy from maximum likelihood; universal-approximation sketch

Part 1. Let $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ be i.i.d. and let $f_\theta$ be any function of $x$. (a) Assume $y_i \mid x_i \sim \mathcal N(f_\theta(x_i), \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ known. Write the log-likelihood of $\theta$, show that its maximiser is the minimiser of $\sum_i (y_i - f_\theta(x_i))^2$, and state exactly which terms were dropped and why they may be dropped. (b) Assume $y_i \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ with $P(y_i = k \mid x_i) = \mathrm{softmax}(f_\theta(x_i))_k$. Write the negative log-likelihood, show it equals the cross-entropy between the one-hot empirical distribution and the model, and compute $\partial L / \partial z$ for the logits $z = f_\theta(x_i)$ of one example. (c) State what changes in (a) if $\sigma$ is a second network output $\sigma_\theta(x)$.

Part 2. Give a sketch, in no more than a page, that a single-hidden-layer network with a sigmoid or ReLU activation can approximate any continuous $g: [0,1] \to \mathbb R$ uniformly to within $\varepsilon$. State the hypothesis, what quantity you bound, and how the number of units enters. You may use the step-function or bump-function construction; name which one you use and why its approximations are not what training finds.

## Self-check

1. For a Gaussian likelihood with unknown but constant $\sigma$, does the MLE of $\theta$ depend on $\sigma$? Does the MLE of $\sigma$ depend on $\theta$?
2. What is the gradient of the cross-entropy loss with respect to the logit of the correct class when the model assigns it probability $0.9$?
3. Does the universal-approximation theorem imply a one-hidden-layer network of a few hundred units can fit ImageNet labels? What exactly does it fail to guarantee?

## Common misconceptions

- "MSE is the natural loss for regression." → MSE is the negative log-likelihood under a homoscedastic Gaussian noise model; a Laplace noise model gives the absolute error, and a heteroscedastic Gaussian gives a different weighting of examples.
- "Cross-entropy and log-likelihood are different training criteria." → For a one-hot target they are the same number; cross-entropy $H(\hat p, p_\theta) = -\sum_k \hat p_k \log p_{\theta,k}$ reduces to $-\log p_{\theta,y}$.
- "Universal approximation explains why deep networks work." → It is an existence result for shallow networks about representational capacity on compacta; it says nothing about depth, optimisation, sample complexity, or generalisation.
