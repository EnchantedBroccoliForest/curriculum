# Week 21: Scaling book, inference chapters; FlashAttention 2022

## Why this week

Inference cost is set by memory bandwidth more often than by FLOPs. FlashAttention is the cleanest worked example of a bandwidth-bound kernel made compute-bound by tiling, and the scaling book's inference chapters give the arithmetic for when each bound binds. Week 26 prices tokens per dollar on this basis.

## Objectives

- Write the HBM traffic of standard attention and of tiled attention as functions of sequence length, head dimension and on-chip memory size.
- Compute arithmetic intensity for a matmul and for attention, and decide from the hardware's FLOP-per-byte ratio which bound binds.
- Explain why decoding at batch size one is bandwidth-bound and what batching changes.

## Key ideas

- Arithmetic intensity is FLOPs per byte moved from HBM; a kernel is compute-bound when its intensity exceeds the accelerator's peak-FLOP/s over HBM-bandwidth ratio, and bandwidth-bound otherwise (the roofline of week 3). Standard attention materializes the $T\times T$ score matrix in HBM, read and written more than once, so it is bandwidth-bound for typical $d_{\text{head}}$.
- FlashAttention tiles $Q$, $K$, $V$ into blocks that fit in on-chip SRAM, computes the softmax online with running max and sum, and never writes the score matrix; HBM traffic becomes $O(T^2 d^2 / M)$ with $M$ the SRAM size, and the FLOP count is unchanged.
- Decoding one token reads every weight once for very little compute, so it is bandwidth-bound until the batch is large; prefill is a full-prompt matmul and compute-bound. Time-to-first-token and tokens-per-second therefore have different cost drivers.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

HBM traffic of standard vs tiled attention; when bandwidth-bound and when compute-bound

Part 1. For one attention head with sequence length $T$, head dimension $d$, element size $b$ bytes, write the HBM bytes read and written by (a) standard attention that materializes scores and probabilities and (b) a tiled attention with block sizes chosen so that a $Q$ block, a $K$ block, a $V$ block and the running statistics fit in on-chip memory $M$. Also write the FLOP count, which should be the same for both. Give the arithmetic intensity of each.

Part 2. Take an accelerator with stated peak bf16 FLOP/s and HBM bandwidth (look them up; label them). Compute the ridge-point intensity. Decide for $T \in \{1\text{k}, 8\text{k}, 128\text{k}\}$ and $d = 128$ whether each of (a) and (b) is compute- or bandwidth-bound. Then do the same for single-sequence decoding of a 7B model (one token step, weights read once) and for the same step at batch 64.

## Common misconceptions

- "FlashAttention is an approximation." → It computes exact attention; the saving is in memory traffic and in not materializing the score matrix.
- "Inference cost is proportional to FLOPs." → At small batch it is proportional to bytes moved; FLOPs matter in prefill and at large batch.
