# Week 23: CS336: data; evaluation

## Why this week

Pretraining data and evaluation are where most published claims about model quality are made and most are weakest. This week supplies the vocabulary (filtering pipelines, deduplication, decontamination, benchmark construction) and the first draft of the benchmark critique that [week 39](/week/39) finishes. The three-way agent comparison in weeks 37–38 reuses the same evaluation discipline.

## Objectives

- Given a data-filtering claim from the FineWeb or DCLM report, say whether it rests on a controlled ablation (same token budget, same model, one variable changed) or on a correlation.
- State what HELM measures that a single-number leaderboard does not, and what it still cannot measure.
- Produce a one-page benchmark critique naming the construct, the contamination risk, and the judge for one benchmark used in a current technical report.

## Key ideas

- A filtering ablation is controlled only if the token budget, model size, training recipe and evaluation set are held fixed while one filter changes; otherwise improvements confound data quality with data quantity.
- Decontamination by n-gram overlap misses paraphrases and translations and flags common phrasing; the contamination study you read should state its detection rule and its miss rate. Quality classifiers shift scores for reasons that need not be general capability.
- HELM's contribution is the scenario × metric grid and standardised prompting; its limit is that scenarios remain fixed text tasks scored mostly by exact match. Harnesses differ in prompt format, few-shot selection and answer extraction, so numbers are comparable only within one harness configuration; with $n$ items and accuracy $p$ the standard error is $\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$, and many reported gaps sit inside it.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

Which filtering claims have controlled ablations; benchmark critique, first draft

Part 1. From the FineWeb and DCLM reports, list every claim of the form "filter $F$ improves downstream performance". For each, record the token budget of the ablation runs, whether baseline and filtered runs saw the same number of tokens, the model size, the evaluation suite and the number of seeds; mark the claim controlled, partially controlled (one confound) or uncontrolled. Part 2. Pick one benchmark from a current open-weight technical report and write a first-draft critique: the construct it claims to measure and whether the items measure it; how and by whom the labels were produced; contamination risk given release date and web presence; the scoring rule and its failure modes; reported variance, if any. End with one sentence on what a score increase would and would not license you to conclude.

## Self-check

1. A report says filter A beat the baseline by 2 points on an average of 10 benchmarks. What three facts about the ablation do you need before the 2 points means anything?
2. Why does changing the few-shot examples or the answer-extraction regex in a harness change scores, and why does that matter for cross-paper comparison?
3. For a benchmark released before the training-data cutoff, what detection method would you accept as evidence of no contamination, and what would it miss?

## Common misconceptions

- "Higher benchmark average means a better pretraining dataset" → Only under matched tokens, matched model and matched evaluation, and only for the constructs those benchmarks measure.
- "Decontaminated means the test items were not in training" → It means items matching one detection rule were removed; paraphrased or translated items pass the rule.
