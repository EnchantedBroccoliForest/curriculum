## Key ideas

- A finite MDP is $(\mathcal S, \mathcal A, p, r, \gamma)$ with transition kernel $p(s' \mid s, a)$, reward $r(s,a)$ (or $r(s,a,s')$), and discount $\gamma \in [0,1)$ or an episodic horizon. A stationary policy is $\pi(a \mid s)$.
- The return is $G_t = \sum_{k \ge 0} \gamma^k R_{t+k+1}$; the state value is $v_\pi(s) = \mathbb E_\pi[G_t \mid S_t = s]$ and the action value $q_\pi(s,a) = \mathbb E_\pi[G_t \mid S_t=s, A_t=a]$.
- Bellman expectation: $v_\pi(s) = \sum_a \pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s'} p(s'\mid s,a)\,[r + \gamma v_\pi(s')]$. It is a linear system in $v_\pi$; for $\gamma<1$ the Bellman operator is a $\gamma$-contraction in the sup norm, so the fixed point is unique and iteration converges.
- Bellman optimality: $v_*(s) = \max_a \sum_{s'} p(s'\mid s,a)[r + \gamma v_*(s')]$; the optimality operator is also a contraction but nonlinear, so $v_*$ is not found by solving a linear system.
- Policy iteration alternates exact evaluation of $\pi_k$ with greedy improvement $\pi_{k+1}(s) = \arg\max_a q_{\pi_k}(s,a)$. The policy improvement theorem gives $v_{\pi_{k+1}} \ge v_{\pi_k}$ pointwise, and finiteness of the policy space gives termination.
- Value iteration truncates evaluation to one sweep; it is the fixed-point iteration of the optimality operator. Generalized policy iteration covers everything in between.
- Prince ch. 19 recasts the same objects for deep RL: the value function becomes a network, the policy becomes a network, and the Bellman equation becomes a regression target that moves. Which guarantees survive the function approximation is a question, not a given.
- Exploration versus exploitation (Sutton and Barto ch. 2) is the bandit special case: no state transitions, so the whole difficulty is estimation under a data distribution you choose.

## Derivation or exercise, stated in full

MDP, Bellman equations; policy iteration on a toy MDP by hand

Part 1. Write down, from memory and with explicit conditioning, the definitions of $G_t$, $v_\pi$, $q_\pi$, $v_*$, $q_*$ and the four Bellman equations (expectation and optimality, for $v$ and for $q$) for a finite discounted MDP with $\gamma<1$. State the relationship between $v_\pi$ and $q_\pi$ in both directions.

Part 2. Construct a toy MDP with at most four states and two actions, with at least one stochastic transition and one state where the greedy action under the uniform random policy is not optimal. Choose $\gamma = 0.9$. Give the full transition table.

Part 3. Starting from the uniform random policy, run policy iteration by hand: evaluate the policy exactly (solve the linear system), improve greedily, repeat until the policy is stable. Record every value vector and every policy. State why the loop must terminate and why the final policy is optimal.

Assumptions: rewards bounded, tabular representation, exact arithmetic (fractions are fine). Do not use code for the solve.

- The termination argument needs two facts: monotone improvement and finitely many deterministic policies.

## Self-check

1. Why is the Bellman expectation equation a linear system while the Bellman optimality equation is not, and what does that imply for how each is solved?
2. In the contraction argument, where exactly does $\gamma < 1$ enter, and what replaces it in the episodic undiscounted case?
3. Does policy iteration still terminate when the policy space is infinite (stochastic policies, or a policy network)? Which step of the argument fails?

## Common misconceptions

- "The value function is the reward." → $v_\pi(s)$ is the expected discounted sum of future rewards under $\pi$; it depends on the policy and on $\gamma$, not only on the state.
- "The optimal policy is unique." → $v_*$ is unique; any policy greedy with respect to $q_*$ is optimal, and ties give several optimal policies.
- "A larger $\gamma$ always helps." → $\gamma$ is part of the problem definition. Changing it changes which policy is optimal, and $\gamma \to 1$ breaks the contraction that makes the discounted theory work.
