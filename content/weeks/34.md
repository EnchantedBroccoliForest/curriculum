# Week 34: Raschka: RLVR and GRPO chapters

## Why this week

GRPO is the estimator behind most open RLVR results since DeepSeekMath, and it is one of the six closed-book derivations (Gate 3, week 40). This week you run it on a laptop and derive its advantage estimator from the policy gradient with baseline of week 15, so that week 35's failure cases (zero-variance groups, length bias) are consequences you can see in the formula rather than facts you were told.

## Objectives

- Run Raschka's GRPO notebook and record the reward curve and pass@k over training, with the group size, learning rate, and KL coefficient used.
- Derive the group-relative advantage estimator from the policy gradient with a baseline and state what the group mean and group standard deviation estimate.
- State two conditions under which the estimator is undefined or biased, with the step of the derivation where each enters.

## Key ideas

- Setup: a prompt $q$, a group of $G$ completions $o_1, \dots, o_G \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot \mid q)$, scalar rewards $r_1, \dots, r_G$ from a verifier. No learned value function.
- The policy gradient with baseline $b(q)$, from week 15: $\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}\big[(r(o) - b(q))\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(o \mid q)\big]$, unbiased for any $b$ that does not depend on $o$.
- The group-relative advantage is $\hat{A}_i = \dfrac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r_1, \dots, r_G)}{\mathrm{std}(r_1, \dots, r_G)}$; the mean plays the role of $b(q)$, the standard deviation rescales.
- GRPO's surrogate is the PPO clipped objective of week 29 with $\hat{A}_i$ in place of a critic's advantage, plus a KL penalty to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$; per-token terms are averaged over the completion's tokens, which is where length enters.
- The baseline is estimated from the same samples it is applied to, so the "any $b$ independent of $o$" argument does not apply directly; the derivation has to say what this costs.
- Binary rewards make the group statistics a function of the number of correct completions $k$ out of $G$; the cases $k = 0$ and $k = G$ are special.

## Common misconceptions

- "The group mean is an unbiased baseline, so the GRPO gradient is unbiased." → The baseline is computed from the same samples it is applied to; the standard independence argument does not apply, and the derivation has to account for the leave-in effect.
- "Dividing by the standard deviation is a harmless normalization." → It makes the per-prompt weight depend on $k/G$; prompts with nearly all-correct or all-wrong groups receive large rescaled advantages, and $s = 0$ groups contribute nothing or are undefined.
- "GRPO needs no reference model." → The surrogate keeps a KL penalty to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$; it removes the critic, not the reference.
