## Key ideas

- Forward process: $q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal N(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$; composing gives $q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal N(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0, (1-\bar\alpha_t) I)$ with $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s \le t}(1-\beta_s)$, so any $x_t$ is one Gaussian sample from $x_0$.
- The reverse process $p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$ is a Gaussian whose mean is parametrised by a network; the ELBO over the whole chain decomposes into a sum of per-step KL terms between $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$ and $p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$, each between Gaussians and so in closed form.
- Reparametrising the mean via the noise $\epsilon$ gives, up to time-dependent weights, $\mathbb E_{t, x_0, \epsilon} \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$; DDPM drops the weights, a design choice, not a consequence of the bound.
- Contrastive objective (CLIP): for a batch of $N$ paired embeddings $u_i, v_i$ normalised to unit length, the loss is the cross-entropy of matching $i$ to $i$ over the $N \times N$ similarity matrix $u_i^\top v_j / \tau$, averaged over both directions. Negatives come for free from the batch.

## Common misconceptions

- "Diffusion models learn to remove noise." → They learn the score, or equivalently the noise, at every noise level; sampling is a sequence of small denoising steps, and the training objective is a reweighted ELBO.
- "Contrastive learning needs mined hard negatives." → CLIP's negatives are the other pairs in the batch; batch size, not mining, is the lever in the paper.
